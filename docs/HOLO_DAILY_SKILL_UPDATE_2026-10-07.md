# HOLO Daily Skill Update — 2026-10-07

## Skill: Safe Action Intensity Routing

### Goal
Keep action scenes visually dynamic without increasing unsafe direct harm, especially when children or family-oriented characters appear.

### Core principle
Safe Action != Weak Action.

When direct-hit intensity must stay low, increase cinematic energy through motion, camera, environment response, VFX, timing, and sound design instead of increasing injury or harmful contact.

### Action Intensity controls
1. **Approach velocity** — faster entry, chase, dash, flight, lateral movement.
2. **Evasion dynamics** — dodge, slide, duck, jump, roll, shield response.
3. **Camera energy** — tracking, whip-pan, push-in, orbit, controlled shake at impact beats.
4. **Environment reaction** — dust, debris, wind, light flicker, surface reaction, displaced objects.
5. **Energy interaction** — shield deflection, beam clash, shockwave, sparks, magical or sci-fi energy.
6. **Landing / recovery** — inertia-aware landing, stagger, foot adjustment, recovery pose.
7. **Impact sound layer** — synchronize whoosh, shield/energy impact, debris and landing sounds to visible events.

### Minor-safe routing
If a scene contains children:
- Prefer transformation, pursuit, evasion, defense, rescue, teamwork, obstacle interaction, fantasy/robot threats and non-injury energy clashes.
- Do not increase intensity by adding visible injury, blood, painful impacts, choking, stabbing, gunshot injury or prolonged suffering.
- Preserve excitement by increasing cinematic motion and environmental consequences rather than harm.

### Example 10-second action beat
- 0–2s: hero transformation / power activation
- 2–4s: high-speed approach + near-miss dodge
- 4–6s: jump or aerial reposition + camera tracking
- 6–8s: shield/energy clash + shockwave + environment reaction
- 8–10s: dynamic landing + recovery + hero pose / story handoff

### HOLO routing rule
When the user asks for "more dynamic", "stronger action", "more intense", or equivalent:

Do NOT automatically raise violence.

Route intensity to:
`Motion ↑ + Camera Energy ↑ + Environment Response ↑ + VFX ↑ + Sound Impact ↑ + Edit Rhythm ↑`

while keeping:
`Direct Harm / Injury <= policy-appropriate level`

### QA
Before approval check:
- Is body movement physically connected and inertia-aware?
- Does every dodge/deflection have a readable threat and reaction?
- Are camera moves supporting rather than hiding the action?
- Are environment/VFX reactions synchronized to the action?
- Are subjects, faces, costumes and hero assets consistent?
- For child subjects, is excitement created without depicting harmful injury?

Those skills improve movement correctness and continuity.

### Product implication
Expose this internally as an **Action Intensity** control.

Increasing Action Intensity should preferentially increase:
camera speed, movement amplitude, evasion, environment response, VFX, sound impact and edit rhythm — not direct physical harm.

This reduces policy-triggered regeneration and preserves a cinematic result.
